Methodology

Where these numbers come from

Every rate in every PourCost calculator has a source, a date, and a range. This page documents all of it — including the places where the published numbers disagree, and how we resolved them.

The short version

PourCost is a planning tool. It uses 2026 U.S. national average rates compiled from published ready-mix supplier price lists, national construction cost guides, and industry benchmark surveys. It returns a low / typical / high range for every line item, because pretending a concrete project has one correct price is the single most misleading thing a cost calculator can do.

It is not a quote, a contract, a structural design, or a code determination. It will not tell you whether your slab is safe or whether your municipality requires a permit.

Before you rely on any number here: call one local ready-mix supplier and one local concrete contractor. Ten minutes of phone calls will beat any national average, and you can enter their quotes directly into the calculator's price override section to get a genuinely local estimate.

How the range works

Each cost line carries three rates — a low, a typical, and a high. The calculator prices every line at all three and sums each column separately. The result is a range whose width reflects the real variance in that line, not a single number with an arbitrary contingency bolted on.

That means the range widens for a project with more risky line items. A simple pad on flat ground has a tight range. A thick driveway on poor soil with a pump truck has a wide one — and it should, because that project genuinely has more ways to go wrong.

Constants that never change

These are physics and standard trade practice, not prices, so they do not vary by region or date:

  • 1 cubic yard = 27 cubic feet
  • Bag yields: 40 lb = 0.30 ft³, 60 lb = 0.45 ft³, 80 lb = 0.60 ft³
  • Bags per cubic yard: 90 / 60 / 45
  • Coverage per cubic yard = 324 ÷ thickness in inches
  • Normal-weight concrete ≈ 150 lb per cubic foot
  • Ready-mix truck capacity ≈ 10 cubic yards
  • Control joint spacing = 2–3× slab thickness in feet, capped at 15 ft (ACI 330R; ACPA)

Sources

Every figure on this site traces back to one of these, read between October 2024 and October 2026:

  • Published ready-mix supplier price lists — four suppliers read October 13, 2026 (California, New Jersey, Nebraska, Tennessee). These are the primary basis for the ready-mix per-yard range: $127.05–$165.00 for 3,000 PSI and $132.00–$175.00 for 4,000 PSI before tax.
  • NRMCA — ready-mix industry benchmarking, average selling price near $160 per cubic yard, and the recommendation to order 4–10% extra beyond plan volume.
  • RSMeans residential construction cost data — regional labor and material rates.
  • HomeGuide, Angi, Modernize, Thumbtack, and LawnStarter cost guides — 2026 installed per-square-foot ranges. These published guides disagree with each other by roughly 40%, which is why PourCost reports a range rather than a single figure.
  • ACI 332 (Residential Concrete), ACI 318 (Structural Concrete), ACI 330R (Slabs on Grade) — thickness, strength, and joint spacing guidance.
  • International Residential Code R506.1 — the 3-1/2 inch minimum for slab-on-grade floors in habitable space.
  • ACPA — control joint spacing of 24× thickness with a 15-foot cap.
  • Bag manufacturer published yields — 0.30 / 0.45 / 0.60 ft³ for 40 / 60 / 80 lb premix bags.
  • Google Keyword Planner (May 2025 – April 2026) and Ahrefs — the search volume and competition figures used to prioritise the FAQ questions on this site.

Where published numbers disagree

You will notice that cost guides quote installed concrete anywhere from $4 to $14 per square foot. That is not sloppiness on their part — they are measuring different things:

  • Material only vs material + labor vs fully installed with base and forms — three different numbers from the same slab.
  • Small-slab bias — a 129 sq ft basis is not valid for a 2,000 sq ft driveway.
  • Excluded ground work — many guides exclude excavation, base, and demolition.
  • Single-contractor pricing — some figures are one company's own prices by size, not a market rate.
  • Scope ambiguity — "concrete slab" with no stated thickness, finish, or reinforcement is not a comparable line item.

PourCost resolves this by always stating the scope and always showing the line items, so you can compare our output against a quote line for line.

How to make the estimate match your quote

  1. Enter the real dimensions in the calculator, including the number of identical slabs if you have more than one.
  2. Set your region from the dropdown — ready-mix and labor both shift by region.
  3. Override the prices with what your supplier and contractor actually quoted. The price override section is on the main calculator under "Override prices with your own quotes".
  4. Toggle the scope to match what your quote includes: base, site prep, curing compound, pump truck.
  5. Compare the line items, not the total. If a number is off, you know exactly which part of the scope to discuss.

What this site will not do

  • It will not size a slab for a load. That is an engineer's job.
  • It will not tell you what your municipality requires. Driveway thickness, permits, and inspections are local.
  • It will not replace three quotes.
  • It will not tell you the mix is right for freeze-thaw. Spec 4,000 PSI with air entrainment per ACI 332, or ask your engineer.
